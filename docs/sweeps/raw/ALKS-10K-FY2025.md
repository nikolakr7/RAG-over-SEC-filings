### Marketed proprietary products (brand → compound)
- **VIVITROL** (naltrexone for extended-release injectable suspension) — alcohol dependence; prevention of relapse to opioid dependence; U.S.; uses polymer-based microsphere injectable extended-release technology; manufactured and commercialized exclusively by Alkermes (line 2340).

## RELATIONSHIPS

### Janssen — NANOCRYSTAL exclusive license (long-acting INVEGA products)

## NOTABLE

### Five most concrete named risk factors
1. **Avadel integration risk** — may fail to realize benefits/synergies of the Feb 2026 Avadel Acquisition; LUMRYZ commercialization could falter (lines 3264-3265).
2. **VIVITROL generic entry in 2027** — last VIVITROL patent expires 2029; Teva licensed to enter Jan 15, 2027, Amneal thereafter; company expects generics in 2027; plus the Value Drug antitrust class action over the VIVITROL patent (lines 4119, 13278).
3. **Licensee dependence / Janssen royalty cliff** — INVEGA SUSTENNA U.S. royalty expired Aug 20, 2024 (long-acting INVEGA royalties fell from $236.5M to $109.6M in one year); remaining TRINZA/HAFYERA royalties end by May 2030; prior Janssen partial-termination arbitration cited as an example of licensee dispute volatility (lines 2411, 3288, 4146-4208).
4. **LUMRYZ is a controlled substance** — sodium oxybate API is Schedule I, product Schedule III; DEA quotas, REMS distribution restrictions; compliance failure could force market withdrawal (lines 2010, 2456, 3346).
5. **$1.525B acquisition debt** — Credit Agreement covenants (Secured Net Leverage Ratio, interest coverage), lien on substantially all assets (lines 3541, 4813-4816). (Also concrete: IRS could challenge Alkermes' treatment as a foreign corporation, and the Nov 2023 Mural separation could fail tax-free treatment, lines 2025-2027; CMS selected VRAYLAR — a direct LYBALVI/ARISTADA competitor — for 2027 Medicare price negotiation, line 3255.)
